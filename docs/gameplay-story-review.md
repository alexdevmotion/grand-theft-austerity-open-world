# Gameplay and story review

Reviewed 4 September 2026 from the campaign, mission runtime, cast director and animation code. This is a source review, not a completed campaign playthrough. Gameplay was left unchanged.

The four-act story has a clear payoff: the player returns to the building that expelled them, restores its lights and joins the community inside. Keep that structure. The biggest immersion gap is how little physical action accompanies the story's most important objects and conversations.

Eight of the 16 campaign objectives are interactions and four are proximity arrivals. Travel can still be exciting because pursuit runs between those markers, but better models alone will not give the player new decisions. After the opening sequence is convincing, test one alternate approach to the broadcast site, with different cover and pursuit exposure. Keep the same objective structure until that route proves fun.

The existing story is contemporary Romanian political satire in an authoritarian setting. `docs/STORY.md` explicitly defines fictional composites. The current realism work should preserve that fiction while using real photos for anatomy and clothing. Communist-era cars, institutional interiors, propaganda screens and worn apartment blocks support this setting without requiring a rewrite into the 1980s.

The most useful next playthrough is short: meet the builders, retrieve the rack, load the Dacia, escape, meet one ally, then drive to the broadcast square. Watch the hands and cargo, character eyelines, subtitle interruptions, pursuit readability and car handling. Record what the player can actually see before expanding the campaign.

For this pass, 48 focused tests passed across character body/animation/IK and story/dialogue/finale restoration. They verify the procedural pivot steps and braking posture, existing body constraints and mission scheduling seams. They do not establish cinematic acting, a finished campaign playthrough or rendered visual quality.
